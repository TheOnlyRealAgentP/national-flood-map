        Statistics

        About this map

        Not a regulatory map. These are modeled flood extents from a national research workflow. They are not FEMA Flood Insurance Rate Maps, do not show flood depth, and should not be used for insurance, permitting, property decisions or emergency response. Local studies, levees, culverts, dams and recent channel changes are not represented.

        What the map shows

        For each mapped watershed (HUC8), the area that would be inundated by the peak flow of a given return period: the 2-year flood (50% chance in any year), 5-, 10-, 25-, 50-, 100-year (1% annual chance) and, where available, the 500-year flood (0.2% annual chance). In All periods mode each place takes the color of the most frequent flood that reaches it, so the darker the blue, the more often it floods.

        How it was made

        1. Terrain and rating curves. NOAA Office of Water Prediction HAND (Height Above Nearest Drainage) data, release , from the public CIROH bucket, run through FIMserv.
        2. Return-period discharge for every National Water Model (NWM) reach, by one of two methods:
          • CIROH ( watersheds): precomputed NWM return-period flows from CIROH's API, 2 to 100 years. Reaches the API does not cover are left out of the flood map.
          • LP-III ( watersheds): a Log-Pearson Type III fit to the annual peaks of the 44-year NWM v3.0 retrospective, 2 to 500 years.
        3. Inundation. The HAND engine turns each reach's discharge into a stage and floods every cell whose height above the stream is below it. Output is a 10 m flood extent per period.
        4. Web tiles. The extents are combined into one national tile set (9.55 m at the closest zoom). Where watersheds meet, a pixel is wet if either side floods it. Zoomed out, a pixel is wet if anything beneath it is, so narrow rivers stay visible.

        Coverage

        • 500-year extents exist only for the watersheds mapped with LP-III. Elsewhere the 100-year flood is the largest shown.
        • Alaska, Hawaii, Puerto Rico, the US Virgin Islands and the Pacific islands: the HUC8s that NOAA publishes HAND for were run, but CIROH has no return-period flows outside the lower 48, so they have no flood extent yet. They show as "Run, no discharge".
        • Watersheds with no NOAA HAND (most of Alaska and the Pacific, Canadian and Mexican basins, open water) cannot be mapped with this method.

        Reading a stream

        Click any stream for its NWM reach: name (from NHDPlus V2 where it matches), stream order, length, slope, upstream drainage area, USGS gage, the discharge for each return period, and the flooded area within the reach's own catchment.

        Data dictionary

        Downloads

        Each watershed's panel links its flood GeoTIFF (one band, a bit mask of flooded periods; GIS software shows it colored by its built-in color table), its reach table (CSV) and its metadata (JSON). The national tile sets are on Hugging Face.
